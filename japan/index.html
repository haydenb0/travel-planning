The Plan

History First, Anime Always

A custom, self-guided trip with private guides on the days where context matters most.

This trip leans into living history — not just seeing temples and castles, but sleeping in them, eating like monks, and picking up a sword. Tokyo bookends the modern side with two days of Ghibli, Akihabara and Nakano Broadway. We chose a custom itinerary over a group tour for pace, flexibility and cost, and skipped Universal Studios Japan (Universal Hollywood is close to home).

Timing. Forum advice for families with teens favors late June to mid-July: July can reach ~40°C (104°F), and Japanese school holidays start in late July, pushing up crowds and prices. June is rainy season but cooler. Every day here starts early and puts museums, shops and indoor stops at midday. Avoid Obon (mid-August). If you're in Kyoto July 14–17 you'll catch the build-up to the Gion Matsuri festival — a bonus, though hotels cost more.

Day by Day

The Itinerary

Early starts, indoor middays, evenings on foot. Days marked 🧭 are good private-guide days.

Tokyo · Nights 1–4

Day 1Arrive

Land at Haneda Travel

Check in, shake off the flight with an evening walk around Shibuya — the scramble crossing at night — then an early night.
Day 2Edo

Edo-period Tokyo 🧭 History

Morning: Senso-ji and old Asakusa before the crowds.
Midday: Tokyo National Museum in Ueno — samurai armor and swords.
Afternoon: Imperial Palace East Gardens, on the grounds of Edo Castle.
Evening: teamLab Planets in Toyosu — the immersive digital-art museum is a staple of family itineraries (1–2 hrs, open until 10pm, timed tickets).
Day 3Anime

Anime Day 1 — Tokyo's west side Anime

Morning: Ghibli Museum (timed ticket), then Inokashira Park next door.
Midday: Nakano Broadway — four floors of vintage manga, figures and collectibles.
Evening: Akihabara — multi-floor anime stores, retro games, gachapon halls and arcades.
Day 4Nikko

Shogun shrine & Edo Wonderland History Immersion

7:00 Tobu limited express from Asakusa (~1h50).
Morning: Toshogu Shrine, the lavish mausoleum of the first Tokugawa shogun.
Afternoon: Edo Wonderland (from ~2pm, when entry is cheaper) — an Edo-period town with costume rental and ninja shows. Reviewers suggest 4–6 hours; shows are mostly in Japanese.
Evening: back in Tokyo around 8pm.
Decision 2Both, or one? Doing both makes a ~13-hour day in summer heat. Alternatives: Toshogu only, or a full, unhurried day at Edo Wonderland. Edo Wonderland is closed Wednesdays (and some Tuesdays) — schedule accordingly.

Hakone · Night 5

Day 5Ryokan

Ikebukuro, then the mountains Anime Immersion

Morning: half anime day in Ikebukuro — the Animate flagship and Pokémon Center Mega Tokyo.
Midday: Romancecar from Shinjuku to Hakone.
Evening: ryokan check-in, onsen, yukata robes and a multi-course kaiseki dinner.

Kyoto · Nights 6–9

Day 6Move

Lake Ashi → Kyoto Travel

Morning: Lake Ashi boat, Owakudani ropeway and the old Hakone checkpoint on the Tokaido highway.
Afternoon: Shinkansen from Odawara to Kyoto, about 2 hours. Settle into the machiya.
Evening: lantern-lit walk through Gion and Pontocho.
Day 7Temples

Shrines, temples & tea 🧭 History Immersion

Dawn: Fushimi Inari's thousands of torii, before the heat and crowds.
Late morning: Kiyomizu-dera and the Higashiyama lanes.
Afternoon: tea ceremony with a kimono experience.
Day 8Samurai

Samurai day Immersion History

Morning: Kyoto Samurai Experience in a former samurai home — training swords, cutting bamboo mats, a short zen session and 650-year-old blades.
Midday: Nijo Castle next door and its squeaking "nightingale" floors.
Afternoon: Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion, at an easy pace, then a rest before dinner.
Day 9Nara

Great Buddha & ninja country 🧭 History Immersion

7:00 Private driver-guide pickup in Kyoto.
Early morning: Todai-ji's Great Buddha and the bowing deer of Nara Park, before the heat.
Late morning–afternoon: Iga-Ueno — ninja house, live show and shuriken throwing. The show runs only twice a day, so time arrival around it, and book the English ninja-house tour 48 hours ahead.
Evening: back in Kyoto. The driver is essential here — by train Iga alone is ~2 hours each way.

Koyasan · Night 10

Day 10Temple

Bamboo, zen & the holy mountain Immersion Travel

Dawn: Arashiyama bamboo grove, then an optional zen session at Shunko-in (English, by reservation) — or a slower morning, since Day 8 already includes zen.
Midday: about 3 hours by train and cable car to Koyasan.
Evening: monastery check-in, shojin ryori vegetarian dinner, and a night lantern walk through Okunoin cemetery.
Day 11Home

Morning prayers, then fly Travel

Join the monks' morning prayers and fire ritual. About 2.5 hours to Kansai Airport — book a departure after roughly 2pm.
Watch the closed days. The Tokyo National Museum is usually closed Mondays and the Ghibli Museum on Tuesdays. Once dates are set, shuffle Days 2–4 so neither lands on its closed day.

Kyoto Samurai Experience

Kyoto · Day 81–2 hrsFrom ~$116 pp★ 5.0 · ~1,480 reviews
⌄
Top-rated for older teens

Held in a former samurai residence near Nijo Castle. Training with iaitō swords, cutting bamboo mats, a short zen meditation, and a look at swords up to 650 years old. Parents describe it as safe, well-briefed and especially good for older teens; professional photos are included. Confirm the minimum age for mat-cutting when booking.

2

Iga-Ueno ninja town

Mie · Day 9Half dayPrivate driver suggested
⌄
Hands-on

The birthplace of the Iga ninja clans. A preserved ninja house with hidden passages, a live show of ninja techniques and shuriken throwing, plus nearby Iga Ueno Castle. The show runs about 25 minutes, twice a day; the English ninja-house tour needs booking 48 hours ahead. Mostly Japanese otherwise. Alternative: the Koka Ninja House, a real ninja family's home about an hour from Kyoto.

3

Nijo Castle

Kyoto · Day 8~1.5 hrs
⌄
Guide adds a lot

The Tokugawa shoguns' Kyoto residence. Its "nightingale floors" were built to chirp underfoot and warn of intruders — walk them and listen.

4

Koyasan monastery night

Wakayama · Day 10OvernightBook Jan–Mar
⌄
Unique

Founded by the monk Kukai in the 9th century, Koyasan is the center of Shingon Buddhism. Stay at a temple, eat shojin ryori, take the guided night tour of Okunoin, and join morning prayers and the goma fire ritual. Many temples also offer meditation and sutra copying. Expect shared bathrooms at most temples, and book months ahead.

5

Tea ceremony & zen

Kyoto · Days 7 & 10~1 hr each
⌄
Calm counterpoint

A tea ceremony with kimono rental, and a zen meditation session led by a monk. Shunko-in in Myoshin-ji runs English-language zen sessions through 2026 (about 2 hours, reservation required, fees on request). Optional, since the Day 8 samurai experience includes a short zen session.

6

Nikko & Edo Wonderland

Tochigi · Day 4Full day★ 4.2 · ~700 reviews
⌄
Long day

Toshogu is the ornate mausoleum of Tokugawa Ieyasu, founder of the shogunate. Edo Wonderland recreates an Edo-period town with ninja shows and costume rental; reviewers praise the immersion but note shows are mostly in Japanese and food is pricey. ¥5,800 per adult (¥5,000 after 2pm in summer). Closed Wednesdays and some Tuesdays.

Decision 2Both sites in one day, or pick one? See Day 4.
Dropped after review: Toei Kyoto Studio Park (3.7★ — reviewers call it dated, with Japanese-only shows). Edo Wonderland covers the same ground better.
Swap options: Kanazawa (samurai district plus the trapdoor-filled "Ninja Temple") or Himeji Castle, Japan's finest original castle, can replace a day if you want more castles.

🚇 IC cards

Add Suica or PASMO to each iPhone Wallet. One tap for trains, subways, buses and convenience stores. Teens 12+ pay adult fares.

🚫 Skip the JR Pass

The 7-day pass rose to ¥53,000 on Oct 1, 2026. This route's long legs cost far less as individual tickets.

🚕 Kyoto taxis

City buses get packed. For four people, a taxi or the GO app often costs about the same per head and saves real time.

Why no rental car: scarce, pricey parking in Tokyo and Kyoto, steep expressway tolls, left-side driving and an International Driving Permit — while the Shinkansen runs center to center faster than you could drive.
Travel Light

Carry-On Only

Station stairs, Shinkansen racks and three hotel moves all favor small bags.

👕 Pack for 5 days, wash twice

  • Quick-dry merino or synthetic tops — skip cotton in summer heat
  • One pair of slip-on walking shoes, worn on the plane
  • Socks without holes — shoes come off at temples, the ryokan and some restaurants
  • A small hand towel each — many restrooms have no paper towels
  • A daypack per person for day trips

🛍️ The souvenir plan

  • Packable duffel: fill it at the end and check it home
  • Ship it: Japan Post box home mid-trip — good for fragile figures
  • Merch allowance: a set amount of bag space per teen
  • Buy umbrellas, cooling towels and fans there — cheap at Daiso and convenience stores
Weigh your bags. ANA and JAL cap carry-ons at about 10 kg including the personal item, and bags get weighed more often in Japan. Backpack-style carry-ons handle stairs and old Kyoto lanes better than spinners.
