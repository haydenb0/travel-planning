The Plan

History First, Anime Always

A custom, self-guided trip with private guides on the days where context matters most.

This trip leans into living history — not just seeing temples and castles, but sleeping in them, eating like monks, and picking up a sword. Tokyo bookends the modern side with two days of Ghibli, Akihabara and Nakano Broadway. We chose a custom itinerary over a group tour for pace, flexibility and cost, and skipped Universal Studios Japan (Universal Hollywood is close to home).

Timing. June to mid-July is rainy season; late July and August are hot and humid. Every day here starts early and puts museums, shops and indoor stops at midday. Avoid Obon (mid-August), when domestic travel peaks. If you're in Kyoto July 14–17 or 21–24 you'll catch the Gion Matsuri festival — a bonus, though hotels cost more.

Tokyo · Nights 1–4

Day 4Nikko

Shogun shrine & Edo Wonderland History Immersion

Morning: Toshogu Shrine, the lavish mausoleum of the first Tokugawa shogun.
Afternoon: Edo Wonderland — an Edo-period theme town with costume rental and ninja shows.
About 2 hours each way on the Tobu line from Asakusa; the two sites sit roughly 40 minutes apart, so start early.

Hakone · Night 5

Day 5Ryokan

Ikebukuro, then the mountains Anime Immersion

Morning: half anime day in Ikebukuro — the Animate flagship and Pokémon Center Mega Tokyo.
Midday: Romancecar from Shinjuku to Hakone.

Kyoto · Nights 6–9

Day 6Move

Lake Ashi → Kyoto Travel

Morning: Lake Ashi boat, Owakudani ropeway and the old Hakone checkpoint on the Tokaido highway.
Afternoon: Shinkansen from Odawara to Kyoto, about 2 hours. Settle into the machiya.
Evening: lantern-lit walk through Gion and Pontocho.
Day 7Temples

Shrines, temples & tea 🧭 History Immersion

Dawn: Fushimi Inari's thousands of torii, before the heat and crowds.
Late morning: Kiyomizu-dera and the Higashiyama lanes.
Afternoon: tea ceremony with a kimono experience.
Day 8Samurai

Samurai day Immersion History

Morning: samurai lesson — sword handling, kembu sword dance, armor photos.
Midday: Nijo Castle and its squeaking "nightingale" floors, then Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion.
Afternoon: Toei Kyoto Studio Park, a working samurai film set with ninja shows.
Day 9Nara

Great Buddha & ninja country 🧭 History Immersion

Morning: Todai-ji's Great Buddha and the bowing deer of Nara Park.
Afternoon: Iga-Ueno — ninja museum, trick house, live show and shuriken throwing.
Book a private driver-guide for this one; it turns a two-stop rail day into an easy loop.

Koyasan · Night 10

Day 10Temple

Bamboo, zen & the holy mountain Immersion Travel

Dawn: Arashiyama bamboo grove, then a zen meditation session.
Midday: about 3 hours by train and cable car to Koyasan.
Evening: monastery check-in, shojin ryori vegetarian dinner, and a night lantern walk through Okunoin cemetery.
Day 11Home

Morning prayers, then fly Travel

Join the monks' morning prayers and fire ritual. About 2.5 hours to Kansai Airport — book a departure after roughly 2pm.
Watch the closed days. The Tokyo National Museum is usually closed Mondays and the Ghibli Museum on Tuesdays. Once dates are set, shuffle Days 2–4 so neither lands on its closed day.

Toei Kyoto Studio Park

Kyoto · Day 82–3 hrs
⌄
History meets pop culture

A working film set for samurai dramas, with Edo-period streets, ninja shows and costume options. It bridges the history and anime halves of the trip nicely.

4

Nijo Castle

Kyoto · Day 8~1.5 hrs
⌄
Guide adds a lot

The Tokugawa shoguns' Kyoto residence. Its "nightingale floors" were built to chirp underfoot and warn of intruders — walk them and listen.

5

Koyasan monastery night

Wakayama · Day 10OvernightBook Jan–Mar
⌄
Unique

Founded by the monk Kukai in the 9th century, Koyasan is the center of Shingon Buddhism. Stay at a temple, eat shojin ryori, take the guided night tour of Okunoin, and join morning prayers and the goma fire ritual. Many temples also offer meditation and sutra copying.

6

Tea ceremony & zen

Kyoto · Days 7 & 10~1 hr each
⌄
Calm counterpoint

A tea ceremony with kimono rental, and a zen meditation session led by a monk. Several Kyoto temples run English-language zazen; Shunko-in in Myoshin-ji is a well-known option.

7

Nikko & Edo Wonderland

Tochigi · Day 4Full day
⌄
Long day

Toshogu is the ornate mausoleum of Tokugawa Ieyasu, founder of the shogunate. Edo Wonderland recreates an Edo-period town with ninja shows and costume rental. Check Edo Wonderland's closed days when dates are set.

Swap options. Kanazawa (samurai district plus the trapdoor-filled "Ninja Temple") or Himeji Castle, Japan's finest original castle, can replace a day if you want more castles. Hiroshima and Miyajima are out for now, pending the teens' interest in the Peace Museum.

🚇 IC cards

Add Suica or PASMO to each iPhone Wallet. One tap for trains, subways, buses and convenience stores. Teens 12+ pay adult fares.

🚫 Skip the JR Pass

The 7-day pass rose to ¥53,000 on Oct 1, 2026. This route's long legs cost far less as individual tickets.

🚕 Kyoto taxis

City buses get packed. For four people, a taxi or the GO app often costs about the same per head and saves real time.

Why no rental car: scarce, pricey parking in Tokyo and Kyoto, steep expressway tolls, left-side driving and an International Driving Permit — while the Shinkansen runs center to center faster than you could drive.
Travel Light

Carry-On Only

Station stairs, Shinkansen racks and three hotel moves all favor small bags.

👕 Pack for 5 days, wash twice

  • Quick-dry merino or synthetic tops — skip cotton in summer heat
  • One pair of slip-on walking shoes, worn on the plane
  • Socks without holes — shoes come off at temples, the ryokan and some restaurants
  • A small hand towel each — many restrooms have no paper towels
  • A daypack per person for day trips

🛍️ The souvenir plan

  • Packable duffel: fill it at the end and check it home
  • Ship it: Japan Post box home mid-trip — good for fragile figures
  • Merch allowance: a set amount of bag space per teen
  • Buy umbrellas, cooling towels and fans there — cheap at Daiso and convenience stores
Weigh your bags. ANA and JAL cap carry-ons at about 10 kg including the personal item, and bags get weighed more often in Japan. Backpack-style carry-ons handle stairs and old Kyoto lanes better than spinners.
